Jacob Helzner, Computer Engineering Master's Candidate

Research Interests

    Computer Perception for Environmental Applications - Segmentation of non-rigid/composite objects, representational invariance, sim-to-real domain randomization. Particularly interested in out-of-distribution (OOD) edge cases in vision foundation models, especially for remote, data-scarce environments.

Academic Papers

  • Synthetic Pretraining for Single-Object Detection in Underwater Environments

    Written for Foundations of Machine Learning. The paper is about how using domain-randomized synthetic data to pretrain foundation vision models can improve performance when real-world data is scarce. The finetuned model learns a prior which speeds up learning from real data.

  • Finetuning a Domain-Specific Chatbot: Trench Crusade using Mistral AI

    Paper for Deep Learning. An application of LoRA to finetune a Mistral-7b on a novel text corpus. Of particular interest are the challenges posed by semantic drift in the limited-data "regime"

  • Planarity Testing and Planar Graph Drawing

    Final project of my undergraduate second course in Algorithms. Discusses planarity testing algorithms in detail and provides a planarity testing algorithm of our own design based on the work of William Goddard.

  • Sweeping Robot in Mujoco

    RL for Robotics Final Project. We used policy gradients to train an arm that holds a broom and sweeps a table. The project revealed relevant problems in computer vision like partial views and visual edge cases.

  • A Note on the Roots of Quintic Polynomials

    A short "practice" paper I co-authored for an undergraduate course in Abstract Algebra. It gives an overview of using Galois theory to show that no simple formula exists for the roots of a quintic polynomial.
